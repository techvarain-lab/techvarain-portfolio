Four things your team can stop chasing.

From the first inquiry to the next review. These four automations keep customers moving while your team focuses on the cleaning.

When a new quote request comes in

Reply in under a minute.

Even at 9pm, the customer gets a friendly response and a link to schedule an estimate. No one has to be at a desk.

Respond while the customer is still interested.

When a quote goes quiet

Follow up automatically.

A gentle check-in goes out a day or two later, followed by another at the agreed time. The sequence stops as soon as the customer replies.

Fewer forgotten quotes. Less chasing for your team.

When an estimate or call is booked

Send the reminders.

Customers get a text or email before their appointment. If they miss it, a follow-up message gives them a simple way to rebook.

Help prevent wasted trips and empty time slots.

When a cleaning job is finished

Ask for a review.

Every customer gets a short feedback request and a link to leave an honest review. If someone reports a problem, your team gets an alert to follow up personally.

More review opportunities. Problems brought to your attention early.

Where good leads disappear

The leak usually happens after the inquiry.

A Dallas homeowner requests a move-out clean after hours. A facility manager asks for a commercial quote. The next few minutes—and the follow-up after that—decide whether either inquiry becomes a real sales conversation.

01 · Response

Replies depend on who is free

Website forms, Facebook messages, texts, and calls land in different places. Busy days turn a fast response into “we’ll get back to them later.”

02 · Follow-up

One message becomes the whole sequence

A prospect asks for a quote, goes quiet, and disappears because there is no consistent second or third touchpoint.

03 · Visibility

No one knows what happens next

Without a clear pipeline, it is hard to see which leads need a quote, a reminder, a call, or a final decision.
